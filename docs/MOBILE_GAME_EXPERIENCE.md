# Mobile match experience

Implemented in the existing Phaser game, 20 October 2026. These changes use the same session, core rules, bots, tutorial, autosave, and PWA entry point as an ordinary match. There is no separate prototype or scripted replacement game.

## From intention to consequence

| Player intention      | Integrated behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start my turn         | Two large ivory dice are one native tap target over the island. Opening rolls explicitly determine the starter. Eligible pre-roll cards remain reachable; an own-turn victory declaration takes priority.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Understand my roll    | Core commits and saves one real result. Its faces settle and travel to the reserved header slot. Producing tiles and up to six own-resource flights use the existing ledger, including city quantities. Exact balances and a short receipt update immediately, including zero-income rolls.                                                                                      |
| Choose my next action | Hammer, trade scales and an original development-card stack occupy stable dock positions after resolution. The dock fades in without moving its hit regions. Required discard, Ahriman, theft and free-road steps take priority.                                                                                                                                                 |
| Build something       | Three compact construction cards show matching piece silhouettes, points or road supply, resource icons, isolated holding/cost numbers, checks and explicit shortages. A nearby reason distinguishes phase, turn, resources, supply and location restrictions. Full text tables and rules remain under Details. Choose a location, inspect the preview, then explicitly confirm. |
| Remember a plan       | Pin a construction type as a personal goal. Its current shortage/readiness appears above the hand. Trade comparisons include both payment and receipt, so gaining wool while spending the last wood cannot falsely complete a settlement goal. A shortage can open bank trading with that resource selected; the player still chooses the payment.                               |
| Use development       | The deck control opens only the owner's hand. Cards show distinct original emblems and timing labels; selecting one shows its actual effect and required resource choices. Purchases have a separate cost/deck-stock area. Each successful purchase selects the actual drawn card, gives private feedback and allows another purchase. VP cards have no ordinary Play action.    |
| See progress          | Committed resource and score deltas appear near their totals. Personal details show actual continuous-road length, played Knights, public title holders and the owner's hidden points. Title transfers are recorded in public history without revealing private cards.                                                                                                           |
| Set my pace           | Pause → Sound and Motion offers opt-in synthesized sound, haptics where supported, quick feedback and reduced motion. System reduced motion also applies. The interface remains complete when sound/haptics are unavailable.                                                                                                                                                     |

The first-time cue is short and dismissible through the interface guide; a successful human roll also acknowledges it. The guide remains reachable through Game Review and settings. Existing Anahita dialogue and the disposable teaching exercise continue to use their real confirmed-action flows.

## Implementation ownership

- `turnSurface.ts` projects legal pre-roll cards and declaration/roll presentation. `turnMarkup.ts` builds semantic dice, receipt and dock content. `GameSession.roll()` rejects repeated, off-turn, modal-covered and non-roll requests; a second tap cannot become End Turn.
- `constructionMarkup.ts`, `buildFeedback.ts` and `developmentMarkup.ts` consume core costs, placement queries and card restrictions. They never grant rule permissions. `rendering/actionArt.ts` supplies original hammer, deck-back and effect geometry, painted by the existing invalidation-driven Graphics renderer. No new art downloads or dependencies are added.
- `GameHud.ts` measures the large dice before the command and consumes that origin only for the exact matching transition. `GraphicsFeedback.ts` draws pointer-transparent decorative clones. The committed header stays in the semantic tree throughout travel; interruption restores its painted faces immediately.
- `GameFeedback.ts` consumes identified successful transitions once. It bounds own-production travel, applies exact deltas and private reveals, and clears obsolete effects on state, sheet, selected-card, layout, scroll, motion and visibility changes. No animation callback rolls, draws, pays, builds or advances a turn. Legal controls become usable from state immediately.
- `GameAudio.ts` creates a Web Audio context only after a user gesture with sound enabled. It synthesizes short contacts locally, groups feedback by action and releases ended nodes. Seven/discard resolution has no ordinary success chime. Hidden-page suspension and destruction release active audio resources. Haptics are optional, guarded enhancements.

## Layout and accessibility

The normal action layout reserves 104 px header, 32 px passive receipt, 60 px hand, 64 px dock, 52 px primary and 4 px bottom spacing: **316 px**, or 312 px with the compact 56 px hand. Safe insets are applied by the existing shell. A pinned goal expands context to 56 px; an introductory or storage-warning control uses 48 px. Placement retains its separate 52 px destination-list row.

During the pre-roll object, the primary row collapses by 52 px and the board drawing keeps a matching 52 px bottom margin. The inset island projection therefore stays stable across an ordinary roll while the large dice occupy the board's central overlay. Dice travel uses measured client coordinates, never a moving input target. The header reserves a stable result slot before rolling. Mobile layout includes a 320 px-wide variant; visual/device fit still requires the review below.

All interactive controls retain at least 48 px touch height. Native buttons, details/summary and modal focus remain the input adapter. Small board targets retain a full-size destination-list alternative. Persian labels stay runtime RTL text; holding/cost fractions use explicit LTR numeric isolation. Resource, readiness and card-timing meanings have shapes and text as well as color. Disabled construction actions retain a nearby reason. Private purchase announcements name the actual card once per successful transition, even for consecutive cards of the same type. Reloads do not replay a draw or production announcement.

The existing carved-stone material remains, with quieter nested surfaces on costs, card backs and private hand panels. Motion is finite and proportional: a short settle and measured dice travel, 150 ms dock fade, 180 ms sheet fade, brief board placement, and a small actual-card reveal. Quick feedback multiplies durations and bot pacing by 0.55. Reduced motion skips travel/tilt/scale and preserves committed results, stationary deltas, receipts and action access. These timings are tuning choices, not measured evidence of player enjoyment or device performance.

## Persistence and privacy

The optional `buildGoal` field extends the existing version-2 save; old saves without it remain valid. It stores only road/settlement/city/null. Goals never spend resources, select a route or alter bot planning. Practice resets goals and does not overwrite the saved match. Legacy unfinished saves with more than the current 15-road supply are rejected before expensive route evaluation; completed legacy games remain inspectable without computing absent scoring titles.

Sound, haptics, quick/reduced motion and introduction acknowledgement use the separate `sunmark.experience.v1` preference key. Quiet defaults apply to absent, invalid or inaccessible preference storage. No preference or goal update publishes a gameplay transition. Required choices, played-card allowances, private hands and final trade consent continue through the existing save decoder and core invariants.

Opponent inventories and card faces never enter the private-hand markup or public standings. Public card-purchase history does not name the drawn card. An owner's hidden total is visible only in their own projection; normal victory declarations retain the core ten-point/own-turn requirement.

## Verification and remaining device review

TypeScript, scoped ESLint/Prettier and the Node Vitest suite verify the implementation. Regression coverage includes one-roll/rapid-tap guarding, core production, seven gating and reload, pre-roll Knight recovery, declaration priority, repeated private purchases, card timing/privacy, pinned-goal save compatibility, payment-created shortages, real preview/confirmation/score changes, preference storage failures, measured dice geometry, cancellation and reduced/quick feedback. Existing rule/session suites cover theft, free roads, bot turns, title transfers and pending-trade recovery.

Per `AGENTS.md`, browser/device testing, production builds and CI remain with the user. Node checks cannot establish rendered visual quality, native-dialog behavior, touch comfort, audio latency, safe-area fit or phone frame pacing. Before release, review repeated full turns on 320–430 px phones and installed iOS/Android, a long development hand, large text/grayscale, reduced motion, an unsupported haptics device, and pause/reload during each required choice. Measure a representative lower-powered phone and ask returning players about comfort after full matches; no performance or usability result is claimed here.
